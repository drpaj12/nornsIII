## Response (controller, 2026-10-30)

Yes in outline; two corrections change the next experiment.

What round 9 builds (docs/plan/round9-tpeko-real.md): TPEKO-like for CLUSTERING only. TPEKO itself (Cong03tpeko,
ICCAD 2003) builds circuits with a known-optimal timing-driven PLACEMENT; round 9 borrows the idea for clustering.
Steps: real design D -> VPR's clustering C0 -> Step A legalise (6-LUT accounting) -> C; Step B pad to exactly full
(N_C = proven CLB lower bound); Step C strengthen (C a strict local cut optimum under single swaps); Step D remove the
replay channel, and remove combinational loops -> D'. Stock re-clusters D'. No placement step in the construction: the
optimum is proven for area (N_C) and checked locally for cut; placement and routing of C are VPR's own ("an achievable
point, not a bound"), so the WL gap (1.32) and CPD gap (1.03) are relative to one placement of C, not optimal values.

Holes and risks:
1. Seeds cannot rebuild C. Q-N1b: only 25.5% of C's clusters can be rebuilt from any seed. Q-N2: 60% of the CLB gap is
   APPack adding the wrong atom while the right one is already queued, with a strictly lower gain in 99.84% of those
   picks (ties 0.14%). So the data question is not "which seed" but "is there data in the circuit that ranks the right
   atom above the wrong pick" (a group-aware gain term) - that would be the clustering innovation.
2. The construction shapes the circuit (padding, Step C nets, loop removal); features that predict C on D' may be
   artefacts (seed-signal found some: gnd/vcc clusters, fill-dependent features). Guards: the foreign-C control
   (METIS; self-bias ~1.01) and any fix must also win downstream on real designs (E10).
3. Coupling of clustering and placement (Peter's "linkage"): downstream QoR carries placement noise (CPD spread about
   +-3% across designs for a 0.7% mean change) and the placer can compensate a bad clustering. Mitigate: also score
   clustering-only measures (cut, absorbed nets, purity vs C, input pins per cluster); keep downstream speed, WL, area
   as the final test. APPack makes the coupling run both ways (gain attenuated by distance from the global placer);
   not measured yet (flat positions not logged).
4. Speed is outside what the yardstick measures (below).
5. Real designs: room exists on D' (area ~10%, WL ~32%); room on real designs is shown only indirectly (round 5
   synthetic and round 9 real-derived agree); real optima are unknown.
Missed opportunity: Q-N2 logged 26,214 wrong picks with the right atom queued: a ready labelled set for the data
question (features: nets shared with the cluster, 2-hop connectivity (77% of not-a-candidate cases were 2 hops),
word / bit-slice membership, near-critical path membership). Offline, no VPR, cheap.
Proposed next question: Q-N3 "Is there data in the circuit that ranks the right atom above the wrong pick?" (offline
AUC and top-1 on the 26,214 picks, split by design for tune/test). Only if yes: build the gain term, test downstream on
D' and on the real E10 designs (speed, WL, area).

## Why speed is outside the yardstick (and why that matters for APPack)

1. The yardstick's optimum is about area and cut only. N_C is a proven lower bound on CLBs; C is a local optimum of
   the cut (the count of nets crossing clusters, every net counted the same). Nothing proves C gives the shortest
   critical path, so "stock vs C" in CPD compares stock with one point, not with the best possible speed; a packer can
   beat C in CPD (ratios below 1 are possible, and happen on some designs).
2. A min-cut clustering is not a min-delay clustering. Delay depends on which connections ON THE CRITICAL PATH stay
   inside a cluster (fast local routing) versus cross clusters (slow general routing). Minimising the cut can cut one
   critical net to save two non-critical ones; criticality weights nets very unequally.
3. The critical path moves with the clustering: a different clustering re-times the design, so there is no fixed
   target path to be optimal on.
4. APPack optimises mostly for speed: VPR's packer gain is 0.75 x timing criticality + 0.25 x connectivity by default
   (--timing_gain_weight 0.75, vpr/src/pack/greedy_candidate_selector.cpp:648-650), and seeds carry a 0.1 criticality
   weight (blend2, greedy_seed_selector.cpp:89-110). Judged against an area/cut optimum, some of APPack's "wrong" picks
   are deliberate speed choices; the yardstick counts them as errors. That is a bias of the yardstick against APPack in
   area and cut, and it is why speed room cannot be read from it.
5. To measure room in speed we need a known-optimal TIMING clustering, as TPEKO did for placement: construct D' so that
   C also attains a proven lower bound on critical-path delay under a delay model (for example, every critical path
   crosses the minimum possible number of clusters). Parked as a question.
