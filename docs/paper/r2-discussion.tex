Round two. The bitwise idea is tested in four forms, and the evidence is this: word clusters
against module clusters are inconclusive (0.986); laying the words out bit-sliced against the quotient layout of the same
clusters is inconclusive (0.982 in wirelength); the hierarchical
combination is not supported as the best seed (rank 8 of 11); and seeding only the
word cells is not distinguishable from seeding everything (H7d inconclusive, 0.980, better on 9 of 15). The word
membership available from Odin~III covers the flip-flops, adders, multipliers and memories but only
49\% of the LUTs, because ABC's cone provenance merges the spans of
mixed logic, so the word seeds are datapath-register seeds more than whole-datapath seeds; a result
against bitwise seeding here is a result about that coverage as much as about the idea.

Every soft arm of Table~\ref{tab:r2qor}, bitwise or not, random start included, lands between 0.980 and
1.029 of the baseline's wirelength: a band of 4.9\% in a geomean over
15 designs. The conclusions below are conclusions about differences inside that band.

One confound the scale-1 comparison cannot remove: VPR derives its start temperature $T_0$ from the starting
placement, so a start that is worse than the analytic one is annealed hotter and, in these runs, longer. The
geomean start temperature relative to the baseline is 9.24 for the random start,
3.23 for Urd and 4.63 for the best new family, and placement runtime moves with it
(Table~\ref{tab:r2qor}). The arms are therefore compared at VPR's own temperature, not at equal anneal effort. The confound
bears directly on four comparisons: H4 (random start, permuted and quotient layouts have $T_0$ of 9.24, 3.09 and 3.23 times the baseline's, for Urd),
H7a (\code{words} 6.24 against Urd 3.23), H7b (\code{slices} 8.07 against \code{words} 6.24) and H7d
(\code{words\_partial} 4.63 against \code{words} 6.24; against the permuted Urd arm 3.09). The start temperatures
of the arms differ by 3.1 to 9.2 times the baseline's, and the direction of the bias is unknown: a hotter
start can erase more of the seed, or give the anneal more moves to repair a worse one. A random start beating a connectivity-aware start
would be a statement about the schedule as much as about the start. The effort curve (N3) is the experiment that separates the two, and it stays open.

On the coarse-versus-fine question that round one left open, the arms give these numbers: H4 is not supported, with Urd at random 0.997, permuted
1.020 and quotient 1.029. RQ-N1 measures part of the
round-one parity: VPR's final placement has 2.78$\times$ the null's
module compactness and 3.79$\times$ its word compactness (medians), which
is inconclusive as a rediscovery of the hierarchy by the NMI-and-compactness rule, which suggests the placer already groups much of
what a module or word seed would group. Whether the structure of the
netlist predicts where a seed helps is H2, inconclusive (the one correlation that reaches $p<0.05$ is negative: the best new-family gain vs $z(Q)$; it tracks graph size, and with the edge count regressed out the partial $\rho$ is -0.16, $p=0.574$). All of this is $n=15$ designs, median 125 CLBs, one VPR
seed per configuration; the seed-to-seed spread of VPR is not measured here, so differences of about a percent should not be read as effects.
