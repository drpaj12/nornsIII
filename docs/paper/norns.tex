\begin{abstract}
Simulated annealing is the workhorse of FPGA placement, and it starts from an
initial placement that the anneal then destroys and rebuilds. What if the
initial placement carried real information -- the HDL hierarchy a
provenance-tracked front end kept, or the clusters a graph analysis
recovered from the flat netlist? This paper asks whether such seeds improve
placement, and, because the anneal's early phase can erase any seed, at what
initial temperature the seed's advantage dies. The seeds come from Odin~III's
provenance hierarchy and from Loki~III's L2 clusterings (Louvain, spectral,
affinity propagation, k-means); the die-level arrangement of clusters comes
from a cut-weighted quotient-graph layout; and the seeds enter VPR through
its initial-placement and floorplanning-constraint interfaces, with VPR's
stock checkpointing flag (on by default, following RLPlace) enabled
throughout and an off sweep as the control arm. We report
seed retention at scale~1 and at twice each design's crossover, quality of
results against an unseeded baseline, and the largest tested temperature
scale at which each seed still helps -- the recipe a clustering-based
initial placement should be used with. A second round adds bitwise seeds
(Odin~III word membership laid out as bit-sliced runs), a random start and a
coarse-random arm, and two structure analyses that need no placement run; each
hypothesis is reported with its deciding number, and an appendix keeps the
research log. Rounds one and two are over 15 designs with one VPR seed per configuration. A third round asks whether VPR's packing step dilutes the HDL structure, builds a packer (a copy of VPR's
AAPack clusterer with a provenance-affinity gain term) and tests it against the stock packer on 15 designs with three VPR seeds per
configuration, adds the top-level I/O buses as words, and reads the annealed placement against provenance; its decision rules were
committed before any measurement (one later amendment, to the proximity analysis, is recorded). Affinity packing raises word purity without
growing the CLB count, but routed wirelength is slightly worse than the stock packer's and that comparison is inconclusive.

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

VPR's simulated-annealing placer starts from an initial placement and anneals
it: the schedule begins hot enough to move anything, and the early phase
rearranges the circuit wholesale. The question this paper asks is whether the
initial placement can be made to \emph{mean} something, and how much of that
meaning survives the anneal. Gharibian et al. showed that system-level
structure recovered from a flat netlist correlates with placement
locality~\cite{gharibian2010finding,gharibian2013analyzing,gharibian2014identifying}.
Odin~III keeps the provenance link that makes recovery unnecessary for the
hierarchy~\cite{gharibian2010finding}, and Loki~III recovers clusters from the
flat netlist without a language model. RLPlace~\cite{elgammal2022rlplace}
showed that the anneal itself can be improved -- directed moves chosen by a
reinforcement-learning agent and checkpointing that prevents oscillation --
starting from VTR's analytic placement. This paper connects those lines: the
seed carries the structure, the anneal is measured for how much of it the
seed can keep, and the largest tested temperature scale at which the
advantage survives is reported as data, not tuned away.

The contributions are: (1) a seeding methodology -- provenance hierarchy and
four recovered clusterings, arranged on the die by a quotient-graph layout,
entering VPR through its initial-placement and constraint interfaces, with
VPR's stock checkpointing enabled and its off sweep as the control arm;
(2) the crossover sweep, which calibrates the
highest useful initial temperature per design against the best placement
any seed achieves; (3) the retention and quality numbers per seed, with the
full-seed versus inter-cluster-only split, which measures sensitivity to the
intra-cluster order; (4) a second round that adds bitwise (word and bit-slice)
seeds, a random-start and a coarse-random arm that separate the clustering from
the coarse layout, and structure analyses of when seeding helps
(Sections~\ref{sec:r2-method} and~\ref{sec:r2-results}), with the research log in
the appendix; (5) rounds three to seven, which test whether VPR's packer dilutes the
HDL structure, build clustering benchmarks whose optimum is known by construction, remove
a leak from them, and run a placement corpus with a held-out hindsight-clustering gap
(Sections~\ref{sec:r3-method} to~\ref{sec:r7-results}); and (6) round eight, which places the same designs with
nextpnr's analytic and annealing placers through VPR's flat-placement file, per family, with
a run record per run (Section~\ref{sec:r8-results}).

\section{Background}
\label{sec:background}

\subsection{Simulated annealing placement and RLPlace}

VPR places by simulated annealing from an analytic initial
placement~\cite{luu2014vtr7,murray2020vtr8}. The anneal's moves are mostly
random; its early phase can undo any structure the start carried. RLPlace
improves the anneal rather than the start: a K-armed bandit selects among
directed move types (simple moves and swaps, bounded centroid, median,
critical-path moves), and checkpointing periodically saves the best placement
so oscillation cannot throw it away~\cite{elgammal2022rlplace}. Our experiment
keeps the anneal as-is (VPR already ships RLPlace-style checkpointing, on by
default) and changes the start instead; the checkpointing prediction -- that
save-best/restore extends the useful temperature range -- is one of the
hypotheses we test.

\subsection{The seeds}

The provenance hierarchy (Urd) is the module structure Odin~III records on
every netlist object; it needs no recovery. Urd has a single cluster only on
sha and stereovision3, two on diffeq1 and diffeq2 (their one module plus a
flat group), and one per populated module plus a flat group (3 to 23) on the
other designs, so
its signal is degenerate on the two one-cluster designs. The recovered
clusters (Verdandi)
are Loki~III L2 partitions: Louvain, spectral, affinity propagation and
k-means at several $k$, scored in the Loki~III paper~\cite{loki3} against the
known hierarchy. Agent default: Norns builds the Loki L0 graph.json from the
same Odin~III BLIF, because Loki's key.json uses Odin API names that do not
join to VPR atom names; the clustering code is Loki's.
The two seed families differ exactly in the question's terms: provenance is
the structure the designer wrote, clusters are the structure the flat netlist
still shows.

\section{Methodology}
\label{sec:method}

\subsection{Seed construction}

Each seed is a partition of the placed blocks. Its die-level arrangement
comes from the quotient graph: contract each cluster to a node, weight edges
by inter-cluster net cuts, lay the quotient out by a coarse force-directed
solve, and scale it to the die. Inside a cluster, blocks take the seed's
internal order, or a shuffle in the inter-cluster-only arm; the two arms
differ only in that intra-cluster order, not in the coarse cluster layout.

\subsection{Seed construction in detail}
\label{sec:seed-detail}

\textbf{One die, one netlist.} Every seed targets the same die: VPR's
\code{k6\_frac\_N10\_frac\_chain\_mem32K\_40nm} architecture with six-input
LUTs (K~=~6). The netlist is the VTR-dialect BLIF that Odin~III's front end
writes for each design: \code{read\_verilog --nolatches --vtr-compat},
\code{proc}, \code{fsm}, \code{opt}, \code{flatten}, \code{opt -full},
\code{wreduce}, \code{hardmap}, \code{lower}, \code{abc --lut 6},
\code{stats}, and \code{write\_blif --vtr --lut-k 6 --name-style provenance}.
Each configuration is one deterministic VPR run (\code{--seed 1}). The
unseeded baseline run is the comparison point and also supplies the seed
builders: its analytic initial placement (\code{initial.place}) gives every
soft seed legal slots and its fine order, and its \code{final.flat\_place} is
the reference the retention metric uses.

\textbf{Coarse layout.} Every soft seed shares one die-level arrangement, the
quotient-graph layout. Each cluster is contracted to one node and each pair of
clusters is weighted by the number of nets cut between them; the weighted
quotient graph is laid out by a force-directed solve
(\code{networkx.spring\_layout}, fixed seed), and the embedding is scaled onto
the die's logic-slot bounding box. Each cluster then takes the free CLB slots
nearest its laid-out point, nearest first. Clusters that exchange many nets
therefore start close together, and the die fills in cluster-sized groups
instead of a scatter.

\textbf{Fine order.} The soft arm has two variants that differ only inside a
cluster. \code{Full} keeps the baseline analytic order: a cluster's blocks
keep their relative analytic positions when they are assigned the cluster's
slots. \code{Inter} shuffles the intra-cluster order under a fixed shuffle
seed, so only the inter-cluster structure survives; because the coarse
cluster layout is identical in the two variants, the pair isolates the fine
order. The hard arm has no fine-order choice: it hands VPR one
\code{PartitionRegion} per cluster, sized from the full seed's slots and
expanded until it holds the cluster's logic (and nudged to cover a memory or
multiplier site the cluster needs), and VPR's placement orders the blocks
inside each region.

\textbf{Placement macros.} Every seed honours the die's placement macros: I/O
pads never move (their I/O sites are fixed by the architecture),
\code{mult\_36} macros stay anchored on their tile footprint, and an adder
carry chain moves as one vertical run in a single column, head at the top
(VPR places a chain as a direct vertical macro). Chains are assigned slots
before the nearest-first fill, so a chain is never split.

\textbf{The seed families.} The Urd seed is the provenance hierarchy made
explicit: Odin~III writes each block's HDL module into its netlist name, the
seed builder reads that module from the BLIF, and each populated module
becomes one cluster; blocks that carry no module name join one flat cluster.
Urd is one cluster on sha and stereovision3, two on diffeq1 and diffeq2 (one
module plus the flat group), and one per populated module plus the flat group
on the other designs. The rationale is the designer's
locality: modules are the units the designer grouped, and logic inside a
module was written to communicate (shared words, state, control), so a module
cluster concentrates the connectivity the placer is paid to shorten. That is
the hypothesis the Urd arm tests.

The Verdandi seeds are Loki~III's L2 clusterings of the flat netlist:
Louvain, spectral, affinity propagation, and k-means at $k$, $2k$ and $4k$,
with $k$ the design's module count (two where the design has one module).
Norns builds the L0 \code{graph.json} the clusterings read from the same
Odin~III BLIF, because Loki's \code{key.json} uses Odin API names that do not
join to VPR atom names; the clustering code is Loki's. A recovered cluster
approximates the provenance grouping from connectivity alone, so comparing
Urd with Verdandi isolates what the HDL structure adds over the graph.

\textbf{Why a seed should beat a random start.} A random placement scatters
communicating logic across the die; the quotient layout puts each cluster's
logic together and puts clusters that exchange nets next to each other, so
the anneal starts from the structure it is meant to find and refines from
there. The experiment tests that mechanism rather than assuming it, and it
compares against VPR's analytic start, which already respects connectivity,
not against a random placement. Two measurements report what the seed did:
retention, the fraction of a seed's CLBs found inside its seed cluster's
region at exit, has medians 0.68 (full) and 0.70 (inter) at VPR's own start
temperature and 0.52--0.74 per family at twice the crossover; and the
crossover sweep finds the largest tested temperature scale at which the seed
still wins, the point where its contribution stops surviving the anneal.

\textbf{What the HDL constructs say about a good start.} HDL modules, buses,
datapath words and finite-state machines group logic that communicates: a bus
is one word fanned out to every consumer, a datapath chain is a dataflow
line, and an FSM's next-state logic and registers exchange signals every
cycle. Provenance preserves that grouping in the netlist, so a seed built
from it starts the placer with the designer's locality already on the die;
L2 clusters try to rediscover it from connectivity alone, and this
experiment measures how well that substitute places, not how closely the
clusters match the modules. That is why Urd
and Verdandi are the arms, and why the paper asks whether recovered structure
is interchangeable with provenance.

\subsection{Injection}

Seeds enter VPR two ways, both already in the pinned build (v8.0.0+18416):
soft, as an initial placement file (\code{--read\_initial\_place\_file}) that
the anneal is free to move away from; and hard, as floorplanning constraints
(\code{--read\_vpr\_constraints}) with one PartitionRegion per cluster, which
the anneal cannot violate at any temperature. The hard arm constrains the
regions only, so the anneal chooses the fine placement inside each one (one
hard variant, no full/inter split). \code{--write\_initial\_place\_file}
dumps VPR's own analytic start for the baseline study. VPR's placement
macros are respected: I/O pads and the multiplier tiles stay anchored, and
carry chains move as one vertical run. The seeds are built from an Odin~III
provenance BLIF and Loki~III's clusterings of the same netlist, arranged by
a cut-weighted quotient-graph layout with \code{networkx} (spring layout,
fixed seed). Building the seed files also needs VPR's unseeded baseline
placement for legal slots, so each design's baseline run precedes its seeds.

\subsection{The crossover sweep}

The calibration loop follows the data, in order. First, run every seed and
keep the best final placement $P^*$ (and which seed made it). Second,
cold-start a fresh anneal from $P^*$ and sweep the initial temperature
upward; the crossover is the largest tested scale at which the cold start
still routes shorter than the unseeded run at the same scale, a lower bound
on the true fall point when the sweep stops at its cap. The sweep is run once
per design, so $S$ is design-level and identical across that design's seeds.
The temperature is reported as a multiple $S$ of VPR's automatic
start-temperature estimate. Third, run every seed at twice the design's
crossover against an unseeded run at the same temperature: whether the seed
still wins there tests how much of its advantage survives a hot restart. The
checkpointing arm repeats the sweeps with VPR's checkpointing
disabled; per RLPlace, the crossover should move hotter when it is on.

\subsection{Metrics and data}

Per run: seed retention (fraction of cluster CLBs still inside their seed
cluster's region at exit; mean displacement from the seed coordinate), routed
wirelength, critical-path delay, minimum channel width, placement runtime,
and the per-temperature cost curve. Designs are the 15 VTR benchmarks that
fit the development machine, smallest first, one run at a time under the
heavy lock. Every number in this paper traces to a kept record; large data
keeps its scripts.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

Three observations. First, the seed families span a narrow band but are not
interchangeable: provenance (Urd) is the worst full seed in the soft arm (1.029) and the best in
the hard arm, so it is not consistently better or worse than recovered clusters. The full and
inter-cluster-only arms differ by 0.6\% in pooled wirelength
(1.009 vs 1.015); round one has no arm that randomizes the coarse cluster layout;
the round-two permuted-cluster and random-start arms (Section~\ref{sec:r2-results}) measure it. Second, VPR's checkpointing does not separate the crossover in the
measured range: the two sweeps find the same scale on 14 of the 15 designs where both found one, and the control extends it on stereovision0 (control 32 vs 16). RLPlace's prediction that checkpointing extends the useful
range is therefore not observed on these designs; if it holds, its effect is smaller than the
between-design variation. Third, the safe recipe is
to cold-start from a cluster-derived placement at a temperature scale at or below the crossover,
which is a multiple of VPR's own estimate; above it the seed's retention is still high per family
(medians 0.52-0.74 at twice the crossover, individual runs
0.21-1.00) but it wins only 57 of
105 head-to-heads against an unseeded run at the same temperature -- the coarse structure
survives the anneal, the advantage does not.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Seeding VPR's anneal is worth it only as a calibrated cold start: at
VPR's own start temperature the cluster layouts and the provenance hierarchy place at parity
with the analytic start, but from a good final placement they keep winning when the anneal is
restarted hotter, up to the largest tested scale that still wins -- a lower bound on the fall
point where the sweep hit its cap. The recipe is that crossover table,
not a seed-specific tuning: start at or below it, and expect the seed to inform the coarse
placement while the anneal supplies the fine one.
