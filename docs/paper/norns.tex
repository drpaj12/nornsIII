\begin{abstract}
Before an FPGA design is placed on a chip, the grouping the designer wrote down in the source is mostly lost. This paper tests, one hypothesis and one experiment at a time, whether keeping it helps. It contributes (1) a packer, a copy of VPR's own, with a bonus for keeping the pieces of one HDL register together; (2) a yardstick of designs built so that the best grouping is known, which shows that the standard packer uses 22\% more wire than the known grouping over all topologies and 49\% more on meshes and trees; and (3) a finding about method. The packer's first measured gain (wirelength 0.902 of stock) was a leak: the input file already equalled the answer. Re-run with registers that cut across the blocks, the packer used 1.65 to 2.51 times the wire of the standard packer at every register width we tried, and with perfectly aligned registers it used 0.669 of it. Near-critical connections are kept inside tiles more often than connections in general, on 9 designs. A sweep of VPR options adds eight area and speed effects. Starting placement from the designer's grouping, the idea this work began with, has no measured gain. Speed is judged by the critical-path delay and by near-critical paths; power is not measured. Of 62 hypotheses, 14 are useful, 9 possibly good, 21 inconclusive and 17 dismissed; five ideas are untested.
\end{abstract}
