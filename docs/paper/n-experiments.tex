\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}

Each subsection states one hypothesis, the experiment, the result and the verdict. Numbers are from the files named in the comments of this source and in \code{docs/paper/hypotheses.md}. Tests that were not decided by the numbers are \textsc{inconclusive}; the full list, including every \textsc{dismissed} hypothesis, is in Appendix~\ref{sec:log}.

\subsection{Does the standard packer scatter the HDL's registers? (H8, H10a, H9a)}
\label{sec:e-scatter}

\pp{Hypothesis (H8).} The standard packer scatters the words of the HDL across CLBs: median word purity at most 0.80 and median split factor at least 1.5.
\pp{Experiment.} The 15 designs of Table~\ref{tab:benchmarks}, stock packing, word purity and split factor against a null that assigns the same atoms at random to CLBs of the same sizes (50 samples).
\pp{Result.} Median word purity 0.63 and median split factor 4.00; purity is above the random null on 15 of 15 designs. % data/analysis/r3-results.json H8: median_share 0.6255, median_split 4.0, above_null 15
\pp{Verdict.} \textsc{useful}.

\pp{Hypothesis (H10a).} Io-words give a word to LUTs that the front end's words left without one.
\pp{Experiment and result.} On the same 15 designs, the median gain in the share of LUTs that carry a label is 24.1\%, positive on 15 of 15 designs. % r3-results.json H10a median_gain 0.2411, positive 15
\pp{Verdict.} \textsc{useful}.

\pp{Hypothesis (H9a-word, H9a-wordio).} The Norns packer raises word purity with at most 5\% CLB growth.
\pp{Experiment.} 15 designs, three VPR seeds, source word and source word+io, $\beta$ from a grid chosen on the four smallest designs (which read only purity and CLB count).
\pp{Result.} Purity rises by 0.111 (word) and 0.104 (word+io), and by 0.120 and 0.124 without the four grid-choice designs; CLB count change 0.0\%; Holm-adjusted Wilcoxon $p<0.001$ for both. % hypotheses.md H9a-word, H9a-wordio; r3-results.json H9a
\pp{Verdict.} \textsc{useful}, as a mechanism check: purity is not an end metric. (Source module: purity gain 0.000, \textsc{inconclusive}.)

\subsection{How much room is there for a better packer? (H24, H27, H29--H31, H25/H19, H32, H20)}
\label{sec:e-room}

\pp{Hypotheses.} The yardstick is exact: synthesis keeps the construction and the \emph{known} arm reproduces the intended blocks (H24, H27, H29, H30, H31). The standard packer is worse than the known clustering by at least 5\% (H25, H19, H32). The Norns packer is still worse than the known clustering (H20).
\pp{Experiment.} The yardstick designs of Section~\ref{sec:i-yardstick}: 72 designs at the netlist level, 63 of the 72 at the HDL level that the label rule keeps, 105 alignment designs, arms stock, norns and known, three VPR seeds.
\pp{Result.} The known arm reproduces the blocks exactly (mean reproduction 1.000, 189 HDL runs and 216 netlist runs); the alignment manipulation works (Spearman 0.988 between designed alignment and measured purity; purity 0.100 at $a=0$ and 1.000 at $a=1$) and synthesis keeps the construction on 105 of 105 alignment designs. % r5-results.json repro_HDL, repro_NL; r6-results.json keep, repro, align
Stock over known, geometric mean: on the 72 netlist designs CLB 1.073, WL 1.220 [1.168, 1.275] (90\% interval), CPD 1.093; on the 63 HDL designs CLB 1.073, WL 1.219, CPD 1.082; on the mesh and tree alignment designs (14 cells) CLB 1.152, WL 1.489 [1.394, 1.591], CPD 1.181; Holm $p<0.001$ throughout. % hypotheses.md H25/H19, H32; r6-results.json room
The room depends on the topology: the stock WL ratio is 1.42 to 1.53 on mesh and tree and close to 1.0 on chain and random graphs, so the pooled 1.22 mixes designs with room and designs without. % r5-results.tex exploratory paragraph: NL stock mesh 1.423, tree 1.522, chain 1.004, rand 1.019; HDL stock mesh 1.444, tree 1.528, chain 1.027, rand 1.011
For the Norns packer on the 63 HDL designs, WL is still 1.099 [1.057, 1.147] of the known clustering (Holm $p=0.003$); CLB (1.031) and CPD (1.032) are \textsc{inconclusive}. % r5-results.tex H20
These HDL designs include the \code{dp} designs whose word file is the leak (Section~\ref{sec:e-leak}), so H20 describes the packer with the hint; it does not say how much room remains with realistic words.
\pp{Verdict.} \textsc{useful} (validity, H25/H19, H32, and H20 for WL). Limits of the yardstick are in Section~\ref{sec:limits}.

\subsection{How far is the packer from a known optimum on real designs? (H38, round 9)}
\label{sec:e-real}

\pp{Hypothesis (H38).} On real designs made into known-optimum yardsticks (Section~\ref{sec:i-yardstick-real}), the standard packer is worse than the known clustering by at least 5\% in CLBs, wire or delay. The rule is that of the synthetic yardstick: \emph{room} if the geometric mean of stock over known is at least 1.05 and the 90\% bootstrap interval of the mean log ratio excludes 0; \emph{no room} if the mean is at most 1.02 or the interval lies inside [0.98, 1.02]; otherwise inconclusive. With fewer than 12 designs no pooled claim is made.
\pp{Experiment (pilot).} Three designs (ch\_intrinsics, diffeq1, mkPktMerge: 12 to 42 clusters, with memory slices and multipliers), each built with the own and the foreign clustering, three shuffles, three VPR seeds, arms stock and known (108 runs), plus 18 stock runs on the unshuffled \emph{ordered} files. Flow flags and architecture as the synthetic yardstick (VTR commit \code{3c9a4d23b2}~\cite{rose2012vtr,elgammal2025vtr9}). Table~\ref{tab:r9pilot}.
\pp{Result.} The known arm needs 1.02 to 1.11 times the exact CLB count $N_C$ and reproduces 40\% to 80\% of the clusters; the standard packer needs 1.06 to 1.19 times $N_C$. The room is small on two designs and larger on the smallest: stock over known is 1.13 in CLBs and 1.09 to 1.14 in wire on ch\_intrinsics, between 1.007 and 1.075 on the other two, and the delay ratio is between 0.98 and 1.07 (room only on ch\_intrinsics with the own clustering). No design shows the 1.49 of the synthetic meshes and trees. The ordered control has stock purity within 0.06 of the shuffled arm on all six builds, higher by at most 0.04 and lower once (0.80 against 0.85 on ch\_intrinsics own), so file order gives the packer no visible advantage. The wire gap is larger on the foreign build than on the own build for all three designs (by 0.05, 0.03 and 0.01), which is the direction a small advantage to VPR on its own clustering would take, but three designs cannot separate self-bias from design. % data/r9/pilot-summary.json
\pp{Verdict.} \textsc{inconclusive} (3 designs; the rule needs 12). The row is the pilot and not the answer. The comparison with the synthetic yardstick is a pointer only: on these three designs the artificial regularity did not hide a larger gap.

\pp{Full set.} \emph{Plain English.} On 33 real designs the standard packer needs about 10\% more CLBs than the number of clusters $N_C$ of the known clustering, about a third more wire than the placement from that clustering, and a few per cent more delay. The own clustering (the one VPR's packer made) gives the standard packer no visible advantage over the foreign one. Two of the 35 designs (stereovision0, stereovision2) are not in the set: the generator did not finish on the second and was not started on the first.
\pp{Experiment.} 13 VTR Verilog designs and 20 MCNC designs (33), each built with the own and the foreign clustering, three shuffles, three VPR seeds, arms stock and known (Norns packer told $C$), plus an exact arm that packs each cluster alone and assembles the clusters (25 of the 33 designs; the other eight have hard blocks, constants or chains the assembled netlist cannot hold). $N_C$ is a counting lower bound on the CLBs of $D'$; where VPR packs the written file with a few small LUT atoms the generator did not count, or sweeps adders, it is corrected to the demand of the netlist VPR packed ($N_C$ corrected, Section~\ref{sec:i-yardstick-real}). The exact arm covers the easier 25 designs. CPD is observed and not claimed optimal.
\pp{Verdict.} H38 is \textsc{useful} (confirmed as a finding about the yardstick): the pooled stock over $N_C$ in CLBs is 1.10 with Holm $p<0.0001$ and an interval above 1, and the same holds for WL against known and exact. The evidence meets the test of \textsc{possibly good} (Holm $p<0.05$, interval excludes 1), but the label is kept for claims about a packer and this is a measurement of room; delay is \textsc{inconclusive}.

\subsection{Does the packer pay when the words equal the blocks? The leak (H33, H36, H35, H34, H22, H23)}
\label{sec:e-leak}

\pp{Hypotheses.} (H33) With words equal to the blocks the Norns packer beats stock. (H36) Its gain grows with the measured alignment. (H35) It makes WL worse by at least 2\% when the words do not match the blocks. (H34) It beats stock at each alignment below 1. (H22) On the 63 HDL designs of the first yardstick round it beats stock.
\pp{Experiment.} The 105 alignment designs; 14 designs per alignment level (mesh and tree, seven sizes); Norns over stock, three seeds.
\pp{Result.} At $a=1$ (words equal blocks) Norns/stock is CLB 0.870, WL 0.669 [0.627, 0.713], CPD 0.841 (H33). % r6-results.json leak_a1
Below 1 it is worse: WL 2.034, 2.038, 1.731 and 1.218 of stock at $a=0$, 0.25, 0.5 and 0.75 (Holm $p<0.001$ for harm at every level; H35), so no leak-free level shows a gain (H34). % r6-results.json hurt
The slope of the WL ratio on the measured alignment is negative in 14 of 14 cells (median $-1.317$; $-0.786$ on the leak-free levels only; Holm $p<0.001$; H36). % r6-results.json dose, dose_leakfree_variant
The first yardstick round had reported WL 0.902 [0.866, 0.936] of stock on 63 HDL designs (H22). % r5-results.json close_HDL
That gain sits only in the \code{dp} designs, whose word file equals the block partition (purity 0.9998), and is zero in the 27 \code{ctl} designs, where the Norns \code{.net} equals the stock \code{.net} on 27 of 27; the purity gain is zero when the HDL has no vector (median difference 0.000; H23). % r5-results.json leak_HDL, h23; round8-h22.md
\pp{Verdicts.} H33, H36, H35: \textsc{useful}; H33 is an upper bound, because the words equal the blocks, and H35 is a finding about harm. H34: \textsc{dismissed}. H23: \textsc{dismissed} as stated. H22: \textsc{dismissed}: its gain is withdrawn as a result. It was an oracle leak and is reported here only as a finding about method.

\subsection{With realistic words, does the packer reduce wirelength? (H22$'$, H37)}
\label{sec:e-h22p}

\pp{Hypothesis (H22$'$).} With bus words that do not mirror the CLB partition, the Norns packer reduces WL against stock.
\pp{Experiment.} 84 generated designs (Section~\ref{sec:i-leak}): mesh and tree, four word widths $w$, seven sizes (14 designs per width), and chain controls (seven per width); arms stock, norns ($\beta=4$) and known; three seeds; 798 runs, all completed. Two controls ran first: at $\beta=0$ the Norns \code{.net} must equal stock's (28 of 28 chain designs byte-identical), and at alignment 1 on chain Norns/stock must be about 1.00 (1.003 [0.993, 1.013]; both passed). The hypothesis is supported if the WL geometric mean is at most 0.99 with Holm $p<0.05$, and not supported if it is at least 1.00 or its 90\% interval lies inside [0.98, 1.02].
\pp{Result.} Table~\ref{tab:h22p}. The packer raises WL by 65\% to 150\% at every width, and CPD by 33\% to 41\%; Holm-adjusted $p=1.0$ at every width. It reduces the CLB count by 2.7\% and 5.4\% at $w=16$ and 32 (Holm $p=0.0015$, $0.0010$) and raises it at $w=4$ and 8; a CLB result never changed the WL verdict. The chain control fails at every width, so no mesh or tree figure may be read as a gain. The known grouping reaches 0.672 of stock WL on mesh and tree at every width: the headroom is real, and realistic words do not reach it.

\subsection{Are near-critical connections kept inside CLBs? (H17c)}
\label{sec:e-census}

\pp{Measurement (H17a, not a hypothesis).} Over 15 designs with stock packing, the median share of CLB pairs that are series-critical is 4.0\%, shared-input or orthogonal 32.0\%, and series-noncritical or filler 64.4\%. % r4-results.json n17 H17a median_SC 0.0403, median_SI_OR 0.3200, median_SN_FL 0.6443
\pp{Hypothesis (H17c).} Near-critical edges (within 10\% of the CPD) cross CLB boundaries less often than edges in general under the standard packer.
\pp{Experiment.} The 9 designs with at least 20 near-critical hops, routed timing reports.
\pp{Result.} Median rate ratio 0.652 (near-critical over all edges), one-sided Wilcoxon $p=0.027$. % r4-results.json n17 H17c

\subsection{What is the critical heap, and where does it go? (CH-a to CH-d)}
\label{sec:e-heap}

\pp{Plain English.} While it packs, the greedy packer keeps going back to the connections that look nearly as slow as the slowest. We call that set the \emph{critical heap}. The picture is that the packer works the heap, a new heap forms as it goes, and at the end a heap remains whose criticality has been pushed down to a floor it cannot get below. We ask four things: how big the heap is, which HDL constructs end up in it, whether the heap that the packer sees is the heap that exists after routing, and whether anything known from the HDL before packing predicts it. These are measurements. No decision rule was set before them, so they carry no verdict; the hypotheses they suggest are untested ones (Section~\ref{sec:future}).
\pp{Experiment.} The 45 routed runs of the keep sweep (the 15 Verilog designs of Table~\ref{tab:benchmarks}, the E10 base flow, VPR seeds 1 to 3, VTR commit \code{3c9a4d23b2}~\cite{rose2012vtr,elgammal2025vtr9}), each with the 1000 most critical paths kept; the criticality of an atom is the largest criticality of a reported path through it. The pack-time side is a log of the packer's own criticality on the same flags (a census whose \code{.net} is byte-identical to the sweep run in 45 of 45 runs). Constructs come from the HDL operator, block and netlist-detector views of the structure store. Summaries are the median over designs of the per-design mean over seeds, with a 90\% bootstrap interval over designs. In the default flow VPR computes pack-time criticality once, before packing, with one delay for every net between logic blocks, and never updates it (the only call that refreshes it is in the analytic global placer); the first write-up of this study said otherwise and was corrected, and none of its numbers change.
\pp{Result (CH-a, size and shape).} In no run does the report stop above criticality 0.90, 0.95 or 0.99, so the heap is complete. The post-route heap holds a median 4.6\% of a design's atoms, lying in a median of 14 register-bit fan-in cones (11\% of the combinational atoms). The number of reported paths within 0.01 of the worst ranges from 1 to 25 over the designs.
\pp{Result (CH-b, constructs and bits).} With whole-design baselines, carry-chain adders are enriched only at the very top of the heap: 2.18 [1.17, 2.45] at criticality at least 0.99 ($n=11$ designs), against 1.05 [0.51, 1.37] at 0.90. Multipliers are strongly enriched (4.60 [0.96, 8.23]) but occur in two designs. Equality comparators are absent from the heap in most designs (median enrichment 0.00), and multiplexers and relational operators lie below their share of atoms (0.36 and 0.37). Neither word bits nor carry-chain positions show a most-significant-bit effect: the heap rate by position quartile in adder chains is 0.034, 0.044, 0.031 and 0.034.
\pp{Result (CH-c, dynamics).} The packer does take the pack-time heap earlier (Spearman between cluster number and pack-time criticality $-0.35$ [$-0.43$, $-0.11$]), but the heap does not stay put: only 0.23 [0.11, 0.45] of the pack-time heap is in the post-route heap, while 0.76 [0.31, 0.94] of the post-route heap was in the pack-time heap, so about a quarter of it arrives from below 0.90. The floor is high: with wire delay removed the slowest reported path would run at 0.40 [0.37, 0.44] of the real CPD, and wire is 0.63 [0.56, 0.65] of the delay on near-critical paths. A heap always exists, because criticality is relative to the worst path, and the wire share is what placement decides.
\pp{Result (CH-d, HDL hints).} A leave-one-design-out logistic model on construct kind and expression depth predicts membership in the post-route heap at an AUC of 0.49 [0.38, 0.60] (0.5 is no signal); with netlist depth and register and adder type added, 0.57 [0.44, 0.65]. For the pack-time heap the netlist depth alone reaches 0.73 [0.63, 0.79], better than construct kind (0.70). Grouping the atoms of one register bit's fan-in cone covers a near-critical path completely for a minority of paths only (a median 11\% of the near-critical paths lie inside the cone of their own endpoint).
\pp{Verdict.} Measurements (CH-a to CH-d). In plain terms: the heap is small and concentrated, most of what the packer takes as critical is not in the routed heap, and the HDL constructs we can name before packing hardly predict the routed heap. Limits: only the 1000 reported paths enter (a deeper unreported path may exist); word and bit attributions exist only for atoms in a reported path; the construct and keep runs are separate Odin~III runs of the same netlist, with incomplete overlap; two designs hold multipliers; the 20 MCNC designs have no attribution and are not in the study.

\subsection{How does the greedy pick go wrong, and what are the exception HDL patterns? (HP-1 to HP-4)}
\label{sec:e-pick}

\pp{Plain English.} If the packer's idea of what is critical is out of date, it spends its early picks on the wrong atoms. We replay the packer's own log against the routed result and ask where its picks go wrong, and, because the HDL is what we can see before packing, we keep a catalogue of the constructs for which the pick is known to go wrong. A pattern in the catalogue is an \emph{exception HDL pattern}: a detectable construct for which grouping by HDL structure is a bad packing hint. The aim is that each can be found by a detector that runs before packing and mapped to a hint for the packer. Every hint is untested. These too are measurements and carry no verdict.
\pp{Experiment.} The same 45 runs as CH-a to CH-d, no new VPR run. A \emph{pick} is a molecule added to a cluster; the data are the packer's queue, gains and refusals. Summaries as before ($n=15$ designs unless stated).
\pp{Result (HP-1, stale criticality; P1, P6).} Picks made on pack-time criticality of at least 0.90 are 4.3\% [3.0\%, 11.1\%] of all picks, and 81\% [63\%, 91\%] of them are \emph{stale}: they hold no atom of the post-route heap (74\% of seed picks and 82\% of candidate picks). In the other direction 32\% [15\%, 62\%] of the post-route heap is packed in the second half of the cluster order, and 5\% [1\%, 7\%] had a pack-time criticality below 0.50. A related effect: 10\% [0\%, 25\%] of the picks made on a timing gain of at least 0.90 are atoms whose own input pins are all below 0.50, the gain being inherited from another sink of the net; those stay in the heap 4\% [0\%, 17\%] of the time, against 14\% [6\%, 17\%] for picks with a critical pin of their own.
\pp{Result (HP-2, capacity, filler and ties; P2, P4, P5).} A near-critical path (criticality at least 0.90) touches a median 3.7 [3.0, 4.3] clusters against a floor from capacity of 1.9 [1.0, 2.7]; 98\% [82\%, 100\%] of the paths are above that floor, and 98\% [85\%, 100\%] of the distinct crossing edges fall where the earlier cluster closed with all 10 logic elements used: almost every cut is a capacity cut. What fills the cluster is mostly not the heap: in clusters that hold a post-route heap atom, 70\% [64\%, 74\%] of the logic elements hold atoms outside it. The queue gain of the picked molecule equals that of another queued molecule in 35\% [29\%, 45\%] of the picks (60\% within 5\%), and 98\% of exact ties go to the molecule queued first.
\pp{Result (HP-3, forks and fan-out; P3, P7).} At a fork atom (two or more successors on near-critical paths) the clusters split the successors in 67\% [33\%, 90\%] of the cases ($n=11$); 66\% [26\%, 83\%] of the crossing edges sit at a fork or join atom. Nets of more than 32 sinks, which the packer's gain ignores, are almost absent from the near-critical nets (0.0\% [0.0\%, 0.2\%]; one net in all 15 designs), but nets of 9 to 32 sinks, treated as ordinary nets, carry 24 near-critical nets in 7 designs, driven by comparison results, subtract borrow bits, state bits and enables.
\pp{Result (HP-4, HDL words; P8).} Of 165 word groups of at least 8 bits (36 from the Odin~III attribution, 129 from top-level buses) 91\% have under 1\% of their bit pairs tied by dataflow (a net, or one atom in between). The standard packer already puts only 7\% of the pairs of a word in one cluster and spreads a word over 3.8 times the fewest clusters it could occupy. Grouping a word as a unit therefore asks the packer to bind bits that have no dataflow between them; the words with the most tied pairs (5\% to 25\%) are adder words, where the carry chain does tie the bits.
\pp{The exception HDL pattern catalogue.} Table~\ref{tab:exceptions} lists the 11 patterns. The patterns with a measured effect on the pick or the cut are rows 1, 3, 4, 5, 8 and 9; rows 6, 10 and 11 are measured non-problems or rare, and rows 2 and 7 occur in few designs. Frequencies count the 15 VTR Verilog designs only and say nothing about other design families. Every detector uses only the netlist, the Odin~III record and VPR's own pre-pack timing analysis; for row 9 the detector measured here is the census outcome, and its pre-pack form is not yet counted.

\subsection{Does refreshing criticality while packing lower the delay? (H-refresh)}
\label{sec:e-refresh}

\pp{Hypothesis (H-refresh).} The stale picks of HP-1 come from a criticality that never moves. Refreshing the pack-time criticality while packing, after every closed cluster ($k=1$) or every eighth ($k=8$), with connections that became internal given an intra-cluster delay estimate, lowers the post-route CPD against stock without raising CLBs or wirelength by more than 2\%.
\pp{Experiment.} An option in the Norns packer (\code{--norns\_refresh\_crit k}, default 0 = off) runs VPR's own incremental timing update after every $k$-th closed cluster. A connection inside a closed cluster gets VPR's own chain-molecule estimate of intra-cluster routing; a cut connection keeps the inter-cluster estimate. The seed order is recomputed at each refresh; the gains of molecules already in a candidate queue are not refreshed (stock behaviour). Gate: at $k=0$ the \code{.net} is byte-identical to the stock sweep on 35 of 35 designs and 3 seeds (105 of 105; the option absent, 35 of 35). Then 35 designs (the 15 VTR Verilog designs and 20 MCNC circuits), seeds 1 to 3, arms stock, $k=1$ and $k=8$, E10 base flags, kept timing: 315 runs, all routed. Per design the geometric mean over seeds of the same-seed ratio; pooled over designs with a 90\% bootstrap interval; Holm adjustment over the 12 tests. The rule: \textsc{useful} if the CPD upper bound is below 1.000, Holm $p<0.05$, the CPD geomean is below 1 in both suites and CLBs and wirelength are at most 1.02; \textsc{dismissed} if the CPD interval lies inside [0.98, 1.02] or wholly above 1; \textsc{inconclusive} otherwise (the full rule is in \code{docs/plan/refresh-crit.md}).
\pp{Result.} Table~\ref{tab:refresh}. CPD does not move (1.005 [0.990, 1.021] at $k=1$, 1.007 [0.997, 1.018] at $k=8$). The near-critical endpoint counts fall (to 0.84 to 0.88 at criticality 0.90), but no test survives the Holm adjustment (smallest $p=0.20$). Cost: pack time is 1.87 times stock at $k=1$.
\pp{Mechanism check.} Recomputing HP-1 for each arm: the share of heap picks that are stale is 81\% [63\%, 91\%] for stock, 88\% [69\%, 91\%] at $k=1$ (up 3.6 points [0.0, 8.0]) and 79\% [73\%, 93\%] at $k=8$ (up 0.5 points). The refresh doubles the share of picks that are heap picks (7.7\% to 15.8\%; the reason is not diagnosed), but those picks are no better matched to the routed heap. So the packer now sees a moving heap, and picking from it did not help; the criticality moved the wrong way for the hypothesis.

\subsection{What do VPR's options do to area and speed? (E10)}
\label{sec:e10}

\pp{Hypotheses.} Nine one-factor hypotheses around the base flow (the annealing placer, the default ABC script, $K=6$, the \code{k6\_frac} architecture): two other ABC scripts; $K=4$ (which also removes the hard blocks); the EArch architecture; unrelated clustering forced on and forced off; VPR's analytic placer; nextpnr HeAP and SA through the flat-placement file.
\pp{Experiment.} 35 designs (20 MCNC, 15 from Table~\ref{tab:benchmarks}), three seeds, one factor against the base. The comparisons were fixed, no thresholds were. We call a measure supported only if its 95\% design-bootstrap interval excludes 1.000 \emph{and} its Holm-adjusted $p$ (36 tests) is below 0.05.
\pp{Result.} Table~\ref{tab:e10}. The area and speed measures move in different directions. The area-oriented ABC script lowers the LUT count by 3.0\% and raises the CPD by 14.1\%. The EArch architecture lowers the CLB count by 14.5\% and raises WL by 16.0\%. Unrelated clustering lowers the CLB count by 7.6\% with no supported change in CPD or WL. The analytic and nextpnr placers lower WL by 4.3\% to 5.0\% and raise the CLB count by 3.1\% to 4.0\%, with the CPD not decided. Forcing unrelated clustering off changes nothing: all four ratios are exactly 1.000. % data/e10/ratios.csv, docs/plan/e10-hypotheses.md

The last (forced off) is \textsc{dismissed} as a no-op. Measures whose interval excludes 1.000 but whose Holm $p$ is not below 0.05 (for example the LUT count and CPD of ABC script 1) are \textsc{inconclusive}.

\subsection{Does starting placement from the designer's grouping help? (R1, H4)}
\label{sec:e-seed}

\pp{Hypothesis (R1a).} A seeded start gives better placement than VPR's own analytic start when the anneal begins at VPR's own temperature.
\pp{Experiment.} The 15 designs of Table~\ref{tab:benchmarks}; seven seed families (Urd and six recovered, Section~\ref{sec:i-seed}) in a soft arm with the full intra-cluster order or shuffled; one VPR seed.
\pp{Result.} Table~\ref{tab:qor}. Soft seeds stay within a few percent of the baseline: geomean WL 0.997 to 1.029 across families, 1.009 pooled, with the channel width lower (0.947 to 0.994) and the placement runtime 1.07 to 1.09 times longer. % hypotheses.md R1a; norns.tex tab:qor (earlier version); EXP:25-72
Hard floorplan regions are worse (geomean WL 1.088 over 104 runs; R1c, \textsc{dismissed}). The crossover sweep finds a median crossover of $16\times$ (range $4\times$ to $256\times$), and at twice the crossover the seeds win only 57 of 105 head-to-heads against an unseeded run at the same temperature (R1e). The best placement over all seeds, $P^*$, is the best of about 20 placements and no control restarts the anneal with equal effort, so part of the crossover may be selection. A random start is as good as any seed: with Urd the WL ratio is 0.997 from a random start, 1.020 from permuted clusters and 1.029 from the quotient layout (H4, \textsc{dismissed}).
\pp{Verdict.} R1a and R1e: \textsc{inconclusive}. The honest answer is that seeding at VPR's own temperature has no measured gain on 15 designs and one VPR seed, and this is why the paper moved to the packer.

\subsection{Is the hierarchy physical on the chip? (H11)}
\label{sec:e-hier}

\pp{Hypothesis.} CLB pairs of the same word, or of the same module, sit closer on the annealed placement than their net connectivity alone predicts.
\pp{Experiment.} Stock packing, analytic start, three VPR seeds; the stratified mean distance difference of related over unrelated pairs as a fraction of the unrelated distance (negative is closer), against a permutation null.
\pp{Result.} Same-word median $-0.176$ ($p=0.017$; 9 of 12 designs sign-consistent, 12 required); same-module median $-0.188$ ($p<0.001$; 10 of 11, 12 required). % hypotheses.md H11; r3-results.tex
\pp{Verdict.} \textsc{inconclusive}: the pairs are closer, but the count of designs that the rule requires cannot be reached on the 11 or 12 designs that qualify.

\subsection{How much would hindsight gain, and do other placers agree? (R7a, R8a, R8b)}
\label{sec:e-hind}

\pp{Hypothesis (R7a).} Re-clustering according to where the best stock placement put atoms lowers WL$\times$CPD on held-out seeds.
\pp{Experiment.} 1660 VPR runs; the arm is chosen on seeds 1--5 and measured on seeds 6--10; 15 designs, 8 with a chosen arm.
\pp{Result.} Held-out median WL gap 0.987, CPD gap 0.994, CLB gap 1.026; the objective interval lies above 1 on 1 of 8 designs (7 borderline); 7 of the 15 designs are imputed as 1. % r7-abstract.tex; r7-results.tex
\pp{Verdict.} \textsc{inconclusive}: little evidence of room that a better clustering with hindsight would capture.

\pp{Hypotheses (R8a, R8b).} nextpnr HeAP lands where VPR's analytic placer does, and nextpnr SA places with equal or less wirelength than VPR's annealer.
\pp{Experiment.} 13 eligible designs, seeds 4--6 held out, ratios within a family against the reread control, design bootstrap.
\pp{Result.} HeAP over reread: WL 1.001 [0.984, 1.019], CPD 1.027 [1.009, 1.046], WL$\times$CPD 1.028 [0.996, 1.061]. SA over reread: WL 0.947 [0.919, 0.975], CPD 1.003 [0.973, 1.039], WL$\times$CPD 0.952 [0.909, 1.003]. % r8-results.tex tab:r8family; placers.md Section 10.9 as corrected by 10.10
\pp{Verdict.} Both \textsc{inconclusive}: the WL$\times$CPD intervals include 1; rule H0 means the outside placers inherit VPR's hard blocks and pads; neither outside placer is timing-driven.

\subsection{Is there a seed that makes the packer rebuild the known clustering? (H-seed, H-seed-b)}
\label{sec:e-seed}

\pp{Hypothesis (H-seed-b).} For most known clusters there is a seed atom from which stock VPR's growth rebuilds the cluster exactly, and seeding with it closes most of the area gap to the known optimum $N_C$.
\pp{Experiment.} The packer is given the known clustering of a round-9 design. At each new cluster it grows a trial cluster from every atom of the next known cluster with VPR's own gain and legality, undoes the trial, and commits the seed whose trial equals the known cluster (else the best Jaccard). 33 designs, 3 VPR seeds. As T-VPack~\cite{marquardt99cluster} we report speed and density separately, speed first. % docs/plan/q-n1b-true-seed-oracle.md; data/n1b/summary.json
\pp{Result.} 25.5 percent of the known clusters are rebuilt exactly (mean Jaccard 0.61). Stock over oracle, geometric mean over designs with the 90 percent interval: critical-path delay 0.993 [0.983, 1.004] (oracle better on 13 of 33 designs), wirelength 1.028 [1.017, 1.040] (25 of 33), CLBs 1.012 [1.006, 1.019] (23 of 33). The oracle closes 11 percent of the log gap to $N_C$ (1.118 to 1.105).
\pp{Verdict.} \textsc{dismissed}: most clusters cannot be rebuilt from any seed, so the gap is in growth, not seeding. Wirelength and CLBs gain a little (\textsc{possibly good}); delay is \textsc{inconclusive}. An earlier rule-based seed oracle (H-seed) found almost no gap and is \textsc{dismissed}.
