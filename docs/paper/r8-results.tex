\subsection{Round eight results: other placers in the VTR flow}
\label{sec:r8-results}

% Sources: docs/plan/placers.md Sections 9 and 10 (pre-registration, dated entries, Section 10.9 results as corrected
% by Section 10.10), data/placers/main_*.json, fix1_*.json, main_summary.json, main_tables_heldout_seeds456.txt.
% Run records: scripts/runrecord.py (docstring), written by scripts/placers_main.py.

Rounds one to seven use VPR's placers only, and a finding that holds only for them could be an artefact of VPR. This
round places the same 15 designs with a placer from outside VTR and reads it back into VPR's own flow, so that the
router and timing analysis are VPR's for every arm. The protocol (\code{docs/plan/placers.md}) was committed before any
comparison run. Stock VPR needs no change: VPR reads a placement in its flat-placement file format
(\code{--read\_flat\_place}, one line per atom) at commit \code{3c9a4d23b2} (VTR~\cite{rose2012vtr}, VTR~9~\cite{elgammal2025vtr9}).
A converter writes the external placement in that format, and VPR packs, places and routes around it.

\emph{Arms, by family.} The analytic family is VPR's analytic placer (\code{vpr\_ap}), nextpnr's HeAP~\cite{gort2012heap,shah2019nextpnr}
(\code{ext\_nextpnr\_heap}, which includes nextpnr's own temperature-zero refinement) and a control, \code{vpr\_ap\_reread}, that
reads \code{vpr\_ap}'s own final placement back into the default flow. The annealing family is VPR's simulated annealing
(\code{vpr\_sa}), nextpnr's SA (\code{ext\_nextpnr\_sa}, \code{--placer sa --starttemp 100}) and \code{vpr\_sa\_reread}. Every
ratio is within a family: an external arm is compared with its own family's reread control and VPR arm, and no headline
crosses families. nextpnr's start temperature of 1 made its SA a greedy descent in the pipeline gate (a wirelength of 4.03
times the control on one construction); one pre-registered change, \code{--starttemp}, was chosen from a grid of five values on seeds 1 to 3 of three LUT and flip-flop
constructions before any main run, and gave 100. nextpnr runs on a purpose-built \code{k6\_frac\_N10} micro-architecture overlay
in which a basic element holds a LUT and its flip-flop, with ten per tile.

\emph{Rule H0, and what it biases.} Adders, multipliers and RAMs, and the pads, are fixed at the positions of the VPR analytic
placer (\code{vpr\_ap}, seed 1) in both external arms and for every seed. nextpnr therefore places only the LUT and flip-flop logic, around
hard blocks and pads that VPR's analytic placer chose, so the external arms inherit part of VPR's solution, and the
annealing-family external arm inherits it from the analytic family. nextpnr has no timing model; both VPR families are
timing-driven. Every number below carries this caption.

\emph{Design set and seeds.} 15 VTR designs, six seeds (1 to 6). The result is the held-out report on seeds 4 to 6: the median
of the three seeds per design and arm, then a geometric mean over designs with a 95\% percentile bootstrap over designs
(10000 resamples, seed 20261026; pre-registered), and after the slash a two-level bootstrap (designs, then seeds). Two designs
(blob\_merge and stereovision0) needed 20 basic elements per tile to fit, a rule labelled post hoc and for feasibility only; the means are also given without
them (the numbers differ little, see the data files). \code{sha} and \code{stereovision2} are ineligible: \code{sha}
has no legal reread control, and VPR refuses every stereovision2 file from either family. A first report of
H0 legality (13 of 15 designs clean) was wrong, and a re-scan found 18 faulty records of 180 external records in two designs; after the re-runs
of the fix round no record of an eligible design has a moved pad or hard block or an atom without a cell.

\emph{Reading.} Analytic family (Table~\ref{tab:r8family}): with hard blocks and pads taken from VPR's analytic placer, nextpnr HeAP
lands within about 0.1\% of \code{vpr\_ap\_reread} in wirelength and 2.7\% in critical-path delay; the WL$\times$CPD interval includes 1 in the
pre-registered bootstrap (0.996 to 1.061) and in the two-level one (0.972 to 1.087). Annealing family, conditional on H0
(a difference that cannot be separated from the inherited hard blocks and pads): nextpnr SA at a start temperature of 100 has about 5\% less routed wirelength than
\code{vpr\_sa\_reread} (0.947; the pre-registered interval 0.919 to 0.975 and the two-level interval 0.913 to 0.995 both exclude 1; without the two 20-element designs
0.935), with critical-path delay equal within noise (1.003), and the WL$\times$CPD ratio 0.952 has an interval that includes 1 in the two-level bootstrap (0.899 to 1.023)
and just includes it in the pre-registered one (0.909 to 1.003). Neither external arm is timing-driven, and neither shows a critical-path advantage. The
pre-registered rule that no external arm may exceed twice its family's reread control in wirelength did not fire in the main comparison. This round has
no hypotheses with statuses: it is a comparison across placers, not a test of seeding.

\emph{Run records.} Every VPR and nextpnr run of this round writes a \code{mimir-run.json}: the exact commands (paths replaced by
placeholders), the commit of each tool tree with whether it was clean and how it was found, the binary hash and version text, every parameter as a
value with its confidence and source, the seed, a host alias, time and peak memory, and the hashes of the inputs and artefacts. The format is
M\'{\i}mir~III's run-record format (version 1), and \code{mimir import-runrecords} reads it, so the runs can enter the circuit knowledge base with
how each value was obtained. A parameter that was only inferred, such as the commit of a dirty source tree, is recorded as unknown.
