\paragraph{Round seven (data first).} Rounds one to six each tested an idea. Round seven tests none: it runs 1660 VPR placements (15 VTR designs and 12 constructions whose block clustering is known by construction) and reports what the data shows. All runs use VTR~\cite{rose2012vtr}, VTR~9~\cite{elgammal2025vtr9}, commit \code{3c9a4d23b2}. The hindsight clustering is the stock clustering with logic elements moved or swapped between neighbouring CLBs according to where the best stock placement put them; it is written as a VPR \code{.net} and run with packing off, so VPR builds exactly that clustering. The arm is chosen on seeds 1 to 5 and measured on the held-out seeds 6 to 10. The objective is wirelength times critical-path delay, and a gap is the stock clustering over the arm (above 1 favours the arm). On the 8 designs measured held out (those where an arm other than the stock clustering was chosen) the median wirelength gap is 0.987, critical-path-delay gap 0.994 and CLB-count gap 1.026; the objective interval is above 1 on 1 of them (7 borderline, 0 worse). Individual metrics are worse on some designs (ch\_intrinsics wirelength gap 0.894 [0.857, 0.937]). Over all 15 designs the median wirelength gap is 1.000 (critical-path-delay gap 1.000, CLB-count gap 1.000), but 7 of the 15 are imputed as 1: on them no arm beat the stock clustering on the selection seeds (a selection-seed result, in sample); they never ran on seeds 6 to 10, so no gain on them is not a held-out finding. Median channel-width ratio (net0 over the arm; below 1 means the arm needs a wider channel) 0.995 held out, about 0.5\% more width, and 0.917 on the selection seeds, in sample, about 9.0\% more. On the selection seeds, in sample, the median wirelength gap over the same 8 designs is 1.040. On the known constructions the procedure recovers a median of 0.19 of the wirelength gap held out (no gain counted as 0; 0.32 over the 8 constructions where it found one). For the earlier affinity-bias arm only the choice of the arm was out of sample (chosen on seeds 1 to 5, evaluated on 6 to 10); its construction used all ten seeds, a bias that favours a gain, so its finding of no gain on 10 of 15 designs (median wirelength gap 0.989 on the others) holds. The mining baseline already holds net connectivity, criticality, the stock clustering's co-location and placement distance (\code{log\_dist\_stock}, from the \code{net0} runs of seeds 1 to 5, the same placements the hindsight clustering was built from); on held-out design families its AUC for predicting which pairs hindsight co-locates is 0.949 (median 0.965) on connected pairs and 0.952 (median 0.955) on uniform pairs, so the room for HDL features is small (ceiling-limited). Adding the HDL features changes the AUC by 0.0018 [-0.0001, 0.0048] on connected pairs (shuffled-label null -0.0004) and by 0.0042 [0.0011, 0.0065] on uniform pairs (null -0.0002; positive on 7 of 8 designs), a small gain above the null on uniform pairs, and changes the $R^2$ of normalised distance by -0.349 (median -0.041). Everything after the gap is exploratory and carries a shuffled-label null. The gap is a best-known bound made by selection, not an optimum.
