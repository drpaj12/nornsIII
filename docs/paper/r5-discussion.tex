
\subsection{Round five: discussion}
\label{sec:r5-discussion}
At the netlist level the stock packer's wirelength is 1.220 times, its CLB count 1.073 times and its critical-path delay 1.093 times those obtained from the known clustering (statuses: WL supported, CLB supported, CPD supported). The wirelength gap changes by 0.004 (natural-log ratio) per doubling of the block count (median of the cell slopes; status inconclusive). At the HDL level the HDL-aware packer's wirelength ratio to the known clustering is 1.099, against the stock packer's 1.219; the direct comparison norns against stock gives wirelength 0.902 (supported), CLB count 0.961 (supported) and critical-path delay 0.954 (supported). By style, the comparison norns against stock gives wirelength 0.834 (supported) for \code{dp} (the HDL has the vector, and word recovery returns one word per block, so the word affinity file is the block partition on the flip-flops: the favourable case, and partly a leak of the answer) and 1.000 (not supported) for \code{ctl} (no words, so the Norns packer equals the stock packer). H23 compares the purity gain between styles: median 0.000 per pair, p<0.001; the literal rule needs a median of 0.05, and the gain is concentrated in the topologies where blocks are not already packed (see the exploratory topology table), so the status is not supported. Exploratory: the stock gap sits in the mesh (1.423) and the tree (1.522); chains (1.004) and random sparse graphs (1.019) leave no room for a clustering (a chain packs by connectivity; the random graph has no locality). Limits: the optimum is a clustering optimum by construction, the cut is an upper bound on the minimum cut within the stated slack, and ratios are against what VPR achieves from the known clustering, not against a proven placement optimum. The generated functions are random or XOR-heavy, which is not the structure of human RTL (compare the distribution-shift reading of Loki's round three).
