
\subsection{Round five: discussion}
\label{sec:r5-discussion}
At the netlist level the stock packer's wirelength is 1.220 times, its CLB count 1.073 times and its critical-path delay 1.093 times those obtained from the known clustering (statuses: WL supported, CLB supported, CPD supported), on the designs passing the slack rule of entry (c); the literal exactness check (H28) is not supported, so the cut is not shown to be minimal and only the CLB count is exact. The wirelength gap changes by 0.004 (natural-log ratio) per doubling of the block count (median of the cell slopes; status inconclusive). At the HDL level the HDL-aware packer's wirelength ratio to the known clustering is 1.099, against the stock packer's 1.219; the direct comparison norns against stock gives wirelength 0.902 (supported), CLB count 0.961 (supported) and critical-path delay 0.954 (supported). The H20 wirelength result is not robust to size: for $B\ge32$ (n=39) it is inconclusive (geometric mean 1.091, Holm p=0.154). The gain is a leak, not a result about HDL knowledge. By style, norns against stock gives wirelength 0.834 (supported) for \code{dp}, where word recovery returns one word per block, so the word affinity file is the block partition on the flip-flops (purity 0.9998 against the stock packer's 0.9501), and 1.000 (not supported) for \code{ctl}, where there are no words and 27 of 27 Norns \code{.net} files equal the stock files. This round is therefore no evidence of an HDL-knowledge gain beyond the leak. H23 is not supported under the literal rule (median purity-gain difference 0.000 per pair, p<0.001, 51.9\% of pairs positive, n=27), but its ceiling makes that outcome close to forced: in 13 of 27 pairs d is exactly 0, because the stock purity of the \code{dp} design is already 1.0 (chains at small $B$ and all random graphs), and the mean headroom (one minus the stock purity of the \code{dp} design, the largest gain possible) is 0.047, which is about the 0.050 threshold the median must reach. Exploratory: the stock gap sits in the mesh (WL 1.423, CPD 1.184) and the tree (WL 1.522, CPD 1.163); chains (WL 1.004, CPD 0.993) and random sparse graphs (WL 1.019, CPD 1.042) leave almost none. Conjecture, not tested: a chain packs well by connectivity alone, and a random graph has no locality that a clustering could use. \paragraph{What this round establishes.} On clustering-optimum-by-construction designs the stock VPR packer leaves about 22.0\% of wirelength and 9.3\% of critical-path delay on average at the netlist level (geometric mean over all topologies), concentrated in the mesh and tree constructions (WL 42.3\% and 52.2\%; CPD 18.4\% and 16.3\%) and absent in chains and random sparse graphs; the netlist-level wirelength ratio is 1.073 at $B=4$, peaks at 1.301 at $B=64$ and is 1.182 at $B=1024$, so it does not grow monotonically with size. These numbers are against what VPR achieves from the forced block clustering, not against a proven optimum. What the round does not show is that HDL knowledge closes any of it. A non-leaky test of that needs designs where the HDL words do not coincide with the optimal blocks: words that span several blocks, or blocks that mix several words. That is the next question. Limits: the optimum is a clustering optimum by construction, the cut is an upper bound on the minimum cut within the stated slack (and the exactness check is not supported under its literal rule), and ratios are against what VPR achieves from the known clustering, not against a proven placement optimum. The generated functions are random or XOR-heavy, which is not the structure of human RTL (compare the distribution-shift reading of Loki's round three). Sizes stop at 1024 blocks and no dedicated critical-chain design was built.
