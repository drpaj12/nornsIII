\subsection{Round seven: what the data says and what it cannot say}
\label{sec:r7-discussion}
\textbf{Hindsight gap.} The \code{.net} procedure finds a gain on 6 of 11 VTR designs; on those the median wirelength gap (net0 over the arm, in sample) is 1.049,
critical-path-delay gap 1.041, CLB-count gap 1.042. On the other designs it found no gain, which is a statement about this procedure (a greedy move or swap of logic elements within
distance two from one placement), not about the packer. On the known-optimum constructions the procedure recovers a median of 0.35 of the wirelength gap (8 designs with a gap to recover; Table~\ref{tab:r7calibB} by size),
so it falls well short of the construction's clustering and the real-design gap is a best-known reference, not an optimum
(the procedure keeps CLB positions of one placement fixed, leaves carry-chain CLBs alone, and picks the arm and source run by the objective it reports).
Why stock over known is 1.20 to 1.95 here against round five's headline of
about 1.22: round five's geometric mean is over all 72 designs, whose chain and random topologies have no gap (chain 1.004, random 1.019) while mesh and tree sit at 1.423 and 1.522
(\code{r5-results.tex}, topology paragraph); this round uses only mesh and tree, and the flow and the known arm are the same (\code{scripts/r5.py} \code{arm\_run} and \code{scripts/r7.py} \code{run\_arm} both use VPR's default flow and weight 100):
the stock-over-known wirelength agrees with round five's on the same designs within a factor of 0.973 to 1.078 (12 designs).

\textbf{Out-of-sample check (affinity-bias arm).} Choosing the arm on seeds 1 to 5 and evaluating stock over arm on seeds 6 to 10 finds no gain on 7 of 11 designs; on the other 4 the median wirelength gap is 0.980 (in-sample median on the same designs 0.997).

\textbf{Duplication.} The share of duplication candidates within 5\% of the critical-path delay is zero on 6 of 11 designs and the median over designs is 0.0\%; since criticality comes from the 200 reported
paths this share is a lower bound. The upper estimate of the delay all candidate hops would save is 0.0\% of the critical-path delay (median over designs, stock runs); it is an upper bound per hop, because it makes every candidate hop free.

\textbf{Mining (exploratory).} A model with HDL features, tested on held-out families (variant net\_gain, 6 designs), changes the hindsight co-location AUC on connected pairs by 0.004 (median 0.002) [0.001, 0.009] over a baseline with net connectivity, criticality and the stock clustering's own co-location and distance, against -0.000 [-0.002, 0.002] when the HDL labels are shuffled. Table~\ref{tab:r3status}'s result (same word and same module sit closer than the nets predict) re-measured here holds at the levels
same instance, depth 1, same statement, same word (negative median and at most 5\% of the shuffles at or below it); the instance chain has depth 1 in every design, so the instance levels of depth 1 and of the full path are the same relation and deeper levels do not exist. The relations tested are those in
the provenance names and the word file; module type names are not in the BLIF, so ``module'' means the instance path. Peter's bit-level hint is
answered by the rows of Table~\ref{tab:r7coef} and the ``bit'' rows of the model tables. Models are held out by design family (stereovision0 to 3, diffeq1 and 2, and the mk* modules each form a family), so a
sibling never trains the model that is tested on it.

\textbf{Limits.} Best-known, not optimal. One architecture. VTR designs only; the big VTR designs were not run. The pair universe is a sample (all
co-located pairs, a sample of connected pairs and a uniform sample), the evaluation set is the two unbiased samples, and the 200 reported timing paths bound
every criticality used. Nothing here is a hypothesis test with a status; the associations are the data's, with their nulls.
