\section{Discussion and Limits}
\label{sec:discussion}

\pp{What the experiments say.} The designer's grouping is physically real on the chip (H11, \textsc{inconclusive} by the letter of the rule), and the standard packer loses it (H8). A packer that is told the grouping can keep it (H9a), and when the grouping is exactly the right clustering it uses far less wire (H33: 0.669 of stock). But the same packer, handed registers that cut across the blocks, makes wirelength worse by 65\% to 150\% (H22$'$), and the harm falls as the registers line up with the blocks (H37 per level, H35, H36). The known-optimum yardstick shows the room is real: the known grouping reaches 0.672 of stock wirelength on meshes and trees at every register width (H22$'$) and the standard packer is 1.49 times it (H32). The honest reading is that in these experiments the gain appeared only when the hint was perfect: no alignment below 1 showed one, and our packer at $\beta=4$ does not degrade gracefully.

\pp{The leak as a method lesson.} The original gain (0.902 of stock) was real arithmetic on a flawed input: the word file was the answer. The yardstick caught it because it lets us dial the alignment, and because we reran with words that cut across blocks. A packer or placer claim on constructed benchmarks should always be accompanied by a control in which the hint is useless and one in which it is perfect.

\pp{Multi-metric view.} Area and speed move in different directions: the area-oriented ABC script lowers the LUT count and raises the CPD (E10); the outside placers lower WL and raise the CLB count; the packer lowers CLBs at wide words and raises WL and CPD (H22$'$). A result on one metric is not a result on the problem. Speed is judged here by the CPD and, only once, by the near-critical statistic (H17c). Power is not measured.

\pp{The critical heap.} The packer does not see a moving heap: its criticality is computed once before packing, with one delay for every net, so the heap it works on is the pre-pack heap, and 81\% of its picks on that heap hold nothing that is critical after routing (HP-1). Refreshing the criticality while packing made the packer see a new heap, and its picks were no better matched to the routed one (H-refresh). Most cuts of near-critical paths are capacity cuts at full clusters (HP-2), and the HDL constructs we can name before packing hardly predict the routed heap (CH-d, AUC 0.49). The catalogue of exception HDL patterns (Table~\ref{tab:exceptions}) is the list of what a hint would have to handle, and every hint in it is untested.

\pp{Yardsticks.} The synthetic yardstick has almost no Rent growth, so its gap (1.22 in wire over all topologies) is a gap on regular designs. The real-design yardstick (33 designs, H38) shows a smaller gap in wire than the synthetic one on meshes and trees and a similar one in CLBs: the stock packer needs 1.103 times the corrected CLB lower bound (1.108 [1.080, 1.149] before the correction), and 1.265 times the wire and 1.026 times the delay of the known clustering (delay \textsc{inconclusive}). The artificial regularity therefore did not hide a larger gap. The room is real in CLBs and wire.

\pp{Why the changes did not win.} The decision log of the standard packer (Q-N2) places the CLB gap in growth, not seeding: 60\% of it is a wrong add with the right atom queued, and a seed oracle that uses the answer key rebuilds 25.5\% of the known clusters and closes 11\% of the log gap (Q-N1b). The one circuit-only signal that ranked the right atom, cluster atoms within two hops, is weak and falls from AUC 0.687 to 0.612 when the construction's dummy cells are deleted (Q-N3); as a gain term on real designs it moved delay by $+0.7\%$ with 17 wins and 14 losses (H-2hop). The packer's criticality is stale for 81\% of its picks (HP-1), but refreshing it did not lower the delay (H-refresh). Spare room (Q-N5) and duplication (Q-N6), the two methods of Schabas and Brown, were run on the 20 MCNC circuits of their Table~1 and on 15 VTR designs and were slower, not faster: their packing change is $-1.4\%$ to $-2.1\%$ on delay against their $+6.4\%$, and their duplication stage is $-0.6\%$ on the MCNC subset against their $+7.7\%$. Modern APPack (timing-driven, K6 fracturable LUTs, annealing placer) is near what local, answer-free information reaches, and the 2003 results do not replicate on VTR~9.

\subsection{Limits}
\label{sec:limits}

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Few, small designs.} The real designs are 15 small VTR circuits; the seeding baseline used one VPR seed per configuration. Intervals are over this set of designs and not over a population.
\item \textbf{Constructed designs.} The yardstick designs and the H22$'$ words are generated. The word layout of H22$'$ is a random permutation, the extreme of misalignment; real buses may lie between that and the leak, and are untested (Section~\ref{sec:future}).
\item \textbf{The yardstick is a clustering optimum.} It is exact for CLB count and not a proven placement optimum. Synthesis keeps the construction in only 32 of 72 HDL designs under the original label rule (63 under an amended rule), and the netlist-level zero-violation check passes on 37 of 72 (H18, H28, \textsc{dismissed} by their literal rules); the analysis uses the 63 designs the amended rule keeps, and the netlist-level variants chosen after the data are labelled.
\item \textbf{An erratum.} The affinity files of the first packer experiments left out every adder (carry-chain) atom; H9 and H10 describe a packer that did not see carry chains. The later experiments (H22$'$ and the alignment designs) use corrected files.
\item \textbf{Near-critical evidence is thin.} H17c has 9 designs and \textsc{possibly good}; M\'{\i}mir cannot yet recompute it (Section~\ref{sec:mimir}).
\item \textbf{Outside placers.} Rule H0 and the lack of a timing model in nextpnr bias every comparison against VPR's own placers.
\item \textbf{E10.} The verdict rule is post hoc, $K=4$ is confounded with the removal of the hard blocks, and some arms exclude designs where a run failed.
\item \textbf{The yardstick is optimal for area and cut only, not for timing.} A minimum-cut clustering is not a minimum-delay clustering; the packer's gain is mostly timing (0.75 timing weight in VPR's default), so some of its ``wrong'' picks are deliberate speed choices that the yardstick counts as errors. Speed room therefore cannot be read from the yardstick; the CPD gap against the known clustering is against one placement of it, not an optimum.
\item \textbf{Architecture and benchmark scope.} One architecture (k6\_frac\_N10), VTR~9, and 15 Verilog plus 20 MCNC designs (33 or 35 per experiment); the large VTR benchmarks are not used. A different architecture, or an industrial flow, may differ.
\item \textbf{The real-design yardstick} uses a single-swap margin for the cut and a checked, not proved, global optimum; up to 14 cells per build could not be anchored, and for diffeq1 the independent partitioner check is vacuous. The construction shapes the circuit (padding, fill nets, loop removal), so features that predict the known clustering may be artefacts (Q-N3). The heap studies cover the 15 VTR Verilog designs and the 1000 most critical paths of each run.
\item \textbf{Noise.} CPD spread across designs is about $\pm 3\%$ for a 0.7\% mean change; a method needs a gain larger than that on a sample of this size. Pack time rises 5\% to 6\% in some arms and is not counted as a cost in the headline.
\item \textbf{Power} is not measured.
\end{itemize}

\section{Future Work: Untested Hypotheses}
\label{sec:future}

Ten ideas have no result yet. Each is stated with the one experiment it needs.

\pp{U1: a smaller $\beta$.} A smaller affinity weight may keep words without the harm seen at $\beta=4$ on misaligned words. Experiment: re-run H22$'$ on the same 84 designs over a ladder of $\beta$ below 4 with the same rule.
\pp{U2: partly aligned words.} Words that agree only partly with the blocks, may give a gain or a smaller harm. Experiment: re-run H22$'$ with words drawn at a controlled partial alignment on the realistic bus layout.
\pp{U3: real HDL words.} Real HDL words may behave like the constructions. Experiment: run stock and norns on the 15 VTR designs with the word files as written and test WL with the H22$'$ rule.
\pp{U4: crossover versus selection.} The crossover of the seeding baseline may reflect the seed and not selection of the best of about 20 placements. Experiment: an equal-effort control that restarts the anneal from the best of $N$ unseeded runs.
\pp{U5: packing for near-critical paths.} A packer that weights near-critical connections may improve the near-critical statistics. Experiment: per-object near-critical statistics in M\'{\i}mir (the top-$k$ or within-10\% paths) and a packer arm that uses them.
\pp{U6: reserve room on the path.} Holding logic elements open for a pending critical successor may lower the clusters a near-critical path touches (HP-2: 98\% of cuts are capacity cuts at full clusters). Experiment: a packer arm that stops adding non-critical candidates while such a successor is pending; measure clusters per path against the capacity floor, and the CPD.
\pp{U7: bit-slice instead of word-major grouping.} Nine of ten words have no dataflow between their bits (HP-4), so grouping a parallel bus by bit slice may beat grouping it as a unit, while carry-chain words stay grouped. Experiment: word-major against bit-slice affinity per word class on the 15 VTR designs.
\pp{U8: the connecting pin's criticality.} Using the criticality of the pin that connects to the cluster, not the maximum over the net's sinks, may cut stale timing-gain picks (catalogue row 9). Experiment: a packer arm with pin-specific criticality; measure the stay rate of those picks.
\pp{U9: the other catalogue hints.} Keeping the fork atom with the deeper branch, pulling decision-bit sinks to the driver, discounting paths through hard blocks and breaking ties by downstream depth each have a detector in Table~\ref{tab:exceptions}. Experiment: one packer arm per hint on the designs where its detector fires.
\pp{U10: bit-path affinity.} Giving the packer the fan-in cone of each register bit as the affinity file may lower the CPD and the near-critical endpoint counts. Experiment: cone groups at several $\beta$, cones of the deepest 5\%, and a random-group null on the 15 designs, three seeds.

\pp{Parked at close, not run.} Three further items are named and left open. (P1) A timing-aware yardstick: construct designs for which the known clustering also attains a proven lower bound on critical-path delay under a delay model, as TPEKO did for placement, so that speed room can be measured. (P2) The Loki L3 to packer-hints bridge: a hint file derived from the netlist-level structure that Loki recovers, supplied to the packer. (P3) Seed-split duplication: duplication combined with a seed split of the logic. They are parked, not argued for; each needs its own plan before any run.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

This is a closed, negative-results paper. We set out to see whether keeping the designer's grouping through packing, or any cheap change to packing and placement, gives a deployable gain in speed, wire or area. It does not. The standard packer scatters the source's registers and a packer with an affinity bonus keeps them, but the one gain we measured came from a leak, and with realistic registers the packer used 65\% to 150\% more wire. Refreshing criticality, a seed rule, a two-hop gain term, Schabas-style spare room and duplication after placement each moved the geomean delay by between $-2.1\%$ and $+0.7\%$ with wins and losses nearly even, and none beat noise; the two apparent gains from duplication were selections on the scored number. A seed oracle that uses the answer key helps wire by 2.8\% and CLBs by 1.2\% but not speed. The contribution is the instrument and what it measured: the known-optimum yardstick, with controls that expose a leak, shows that room exists (1.103 times the corrected CLB lower bound and 1.265 times the wire on 33 real designs), places 60\% of the CLB gap in one kind of pick, and shows that a modern packer is near what local, answer-free information reaches. The 2003 results of Schabas and Brown do not replicate on VTR~9. The yardstick is optimal for area and cut, not timing, and covers one architecture and 35 designs. Open items are parked, not run: a timing-aware yardstick, the Loki L3 to packer-hints bridge, and seed-split duplication. The run records are archived and indexed in \texttt{docs/plan/STATUS.md}, which also says how to reopen the path.
