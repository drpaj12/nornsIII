\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

This section gives the results as they stand at the close of the work. Each result is stated as a CAD paper states it: the geometric-mean percent win, with the wins, losses and ties over designs where the register has them, and then the strict register verdict (Section~\ref{sec:verdicts}). The full record of each experiment, in time order, with its hypothesis, experiment, result, verdict and pull request, is in Appendix~\ref{sec:records}; every row of the register is in Appendix~\ref{sec:log}.

\subsection{How much room a packer has}
\label{sec:r-room}

The yardstick measures room. Table~\ref{tab:room} gives the standard packer over the known clustering on the two forms of the yardstick. On the 72 synthetic designs the standard packer uses 22\% more wire than the known clustering, and 49\% more on meshes and trees (14 cells), where the designs have most room; on chain and random graphs there is almost none. On the 33 real designs it needs 1.103 times the corrected counting lower bound on CLBs, 1.265 times the wire and 1.026 times the delay of the known clustering, and the own clustering gives it no visible advantage over a foreign one (every self-bias ratio is within 2\%). The delay ratio is \textsc{inconclusive}: the yardstick is optimal for area and cut and not for timing. The room is therefore real in CLBs and wire. Appendices~\ref{sec:e-room} and~\ref{sec:e-real} give the records.

\subsection{Where the standard packer loses}
\label{sec:r-loses}

Four measurements place the loss. The decision log of the standard packer (Q-N2) puts 60\% of the CLB gap to the known clustering in one kind of pick, an atom of another known cluster added while a legal member of the right cluster was queued, with a strictly lower queue gain in 99.8\% of them; 11\% comes from clusters closed with free slots. A seed oracle that uses the answer key rebuilds only 25.5\% of the known clusters and closes 11\% of the log gap (Q-N1b), so the gap is in growth (gain and closing), not in seeding. No feature computable from the circuit alone ranks the right atom well once the construction's dummy cells are deleted (Q-N3: AUC 0.687 [0.662, 0.711] with them, 0.612 without). On the timing side, the packer's criticality is computed once before packing, 81\% of its picks on a criticality of at least 0.90 hold nothing that is critical after routing (HP-1), and 98\% of the cuts of near-critical paths are capacity cuts at full clusters (HP-2). Eleven exception HDL patterns, each with a detector and an untested hint, are in Table~\ref{tab:exceptions} (Appendix~\ref{sec:e-pick}). All of these are \textsc{measurement} or \textsc{useful} rows. Appendices~\ref{sec:e-seedoracle},~\ref{sec:e-q2},~\ref{sec:e-q3},~\ref{sec:e-heap} and~\ref{sec:e-pick} give the records.

\subsection{The packer that keeps the HDL's registers}
\label{sec:r-packer}

The standard packer scatters the source's words: median word purity 0.63 and median split factor 4.00 on 15 designs (H8, \textsc{useful}). The Norns packer raises word purity by 0.111 with no growth in CLBs (H9a, \textsc{useful} as a mechanism check). When the words equal the blocks it uses 0.669 [0.627, 0.713] of the stock wire (H33, \textsc{useful} as an upper bound, because that is the leak). With words that cut across the blocks it is worse at every alignment below 1 (WL 1.218 to 2.038 of stock, H34, \textsc{dismissed}), and with realistic bus words it uses 65\% to 150\% more wire and 33\% to 41\% more delay (H22$'$, \textsc{dismissed}). Appendices~\ref{sec:e-scatter},~\ref{sec:e-leak} and~\ref{sec:e-h22p} give the records.

\subsection{Tool options}
\label{sec:r-e10}

A sweep of nine tool options over 35 designs moves area and speed in different directions (E10; eight \textsc{possibly good}, one no-op \textsc{dismissed}): the area-oriented ABC script lowers the LUT count by 3.0\% and raises the CPD by 14.1\%; the EArch architecture lowers the CLB count by 14.5\% and raises WL by 16.0\%; unrelated clustering lowers the CLB count by 7.6\%; the analytic and nextpnr placers lower WL by 4.3\% to 5.0\% and raise the CLB count by 3.1\% to 4.0\%. These change tool settings and not the packer. The verdict rule was chosen after the data, so none is more than \textsc{possibly good}. Appendix~\ref{sec:e10} gives the record.

Three things stand out. First, no deployable change wins on speed: the best geomean delay win is 0.7\% (H-2hop at $w=0.1$), with 17 wins against 14 losses, inside the noise. Second, the changes that move area or wire move them the wrong way (spare room raises CLBs by 11 to 17\%). Third, the two selections that looked like speed gains were selections: the duplication result of $+1.9$ and $+2.8$\% (setting a) is the best of up to five routed rounds scored on the number it is chosen on, and the same netlists routed afresh are 0.5 and 0.4\% slower; the original HDL-aware gain (WL 0.902 of stock) was a leak. Table~\ref{tab:tried} does not list the sweep of VPR options (E10), which changes tool settings and not the packer; its eight area and speed effects are in Appendix~\ref{sec:e10}.
