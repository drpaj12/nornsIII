
\subsection{Round four: discussion}
\label{sec:r4-discussion}
The census answers Peter's question with data. In the stock packing the pairs that are neither series-critical nor shared-input nor orthogonal are 64.4\% by median (SN+FL, the non-critical remainder: series-noncritical 3.2\%, filler 57.6\%; a series-noncritical pair does share a direct net, so this is not a share of pairs without a net). Series-critical pairs are
4.0\% and shared-input plus orthogonal pairs 32.0\%; Peter's 10/75/15 was an illustration of what the classification data could teach, not a guess to test; these measured shares are the result.
The orthogonal class counts same-word pairs, and words cover about half the LUTs, so it is a lower bound on the shared-input plus orthogonal share. The headline packer changes the series-critical share by -0.003 (median).
Near-critical paths are counted per benchmark (reported paths) in Table~\ref{tab:r4census}; most designs have few reported paths within 10\%, so H17c rests on 9 designs that pass the registered 20-hop filter: by the literal rule it is supported (median ratio 0.652, one-sided Wilcoxon $p=0.027$); under the labelled reading with an n$<$12 clause (not in the registered rule) it is inconclusive (n < 12).
H17d is supported against the registered null, which shuffles atoms (excess 0.072 of series pairs over the null's 0.2\%, observed 7.7\%). That null ignores that the prepacker forces a LUT and its FF into one CLB and that carry chains fill CLBs in sequence. An exploratory null that keeps those molecules as units and leaves the 21.6\% (median) of CLBs that hold an adder as they are gives a null of 5.3\% and an excess of 0.023 (inconclusive by the same rule, p<0.001). H17d therefore says that the packed pairs include forced ones; it is not a measure of clustering quality.
The hierarchy test (N13) is on 15 small designs, and the provenance names expose one level, so it tests the module count, not depth. The round-three affinity files lacked every adder atom; the round-four headline
packing is therefore not the round-three packing, and round three's purity and wirelength results describe a packer that did not see carry chains.
Limits: the depth estimate is a text heuristic; the attribution workaround uses the tool's functions on BLIFs that carry provenance names; the packing is APPack inside the analytical flow.
