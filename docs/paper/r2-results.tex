\textbf{RQ-N7, bitwise seeds (15 designs, one VPR seed each).}
H7a is inconclusive: \code{words} over Urd is 0.986 in wirelength, better on 10 of
15 designs (sign test $p=0.302$).
H7b is inconclusive: \code{slices} over \code{words} is 0.982 in wirelength
(better on 8 of 15, $p=0.791$) and 1.033 in channel width
(better on 6 of 15).
H7c is not supported: \code{hier} ranks 8 of 11 families at
1.014; the lowest is \code{words\_partial} at 0.994.
H7d is inconclusive (no evidence of a difference): \code{words\_partial} over \code{words} is 0.980
(better on 9 of 15, $p=0.607$).
Ties (identical results) are left out of the sign tests: 0 for H7a, 1 for H7b and
0 for H7d. A family with nothing to rearrange reproduces its reference exactly (a design whose words are
all single-CLB, or that has one module): \code{slices} equals \code{words} on 1
design(s), \code{hier} equals Urd on 1 and \code{words\_partial} equals \code{words} on
0.
Seed audit. Rebuilding the seven round-two seed families from the kept word membership with the committed builders gives
seed files byte-identical to the ones VPR ran from on all 15 designs;
so every \code{slices} run used the column-major bit-order fallback of the committed code, and that fallback changes the slices
seed on 6 designs (ch\_intrinsics, diffeq1, mkPktMerge, raygentop, stereovision0, stereovision2). The designs where \code{slices} equals \code{words} as a seed file
(mkDelayWorker32B) and where \code{hier} equals Urd (mkDelayWorker32B) are equal because there is nothing to rearrange, not because
a code path was skipped. On \code{mkDelayWorker32B} only 9 CLBs carry a word label, in 7 words, and no word has two CLBs off the carry chains.

\code{words\_partial} places only the word CLBs, so how much of each design it moves matters for H7d
(Table~\ref{tab:r2partial}). Of the logic and memory CLBs the seed moves a median of 79\% per design
(6 of 15 designs at 90\% or more, down to 15\% on mkDelayWorker32B); counting every placed block, pads and
multipliers included, which are anchored in every arm, the median is only 18\% (for example
11 of 243 on ch\_intrinsics). Where the seed covers nearly all logic CLBs,
\code{words\_partial} differs little from \code{words}, so H7d compares two similar placements; where it covers little (for example
mkDelayWorker32B), most of the seed is VPR's own analytic start. H7d is
therefore a weak test of ``seeding fewer CLBs'' on the designs where the share is high.
The best new family by geomean wirelength is \code{words\_partial} (chosen on the 13 designs whose
soft arms were complete when the hard and crossover arms were queued), so the coarse-random, hard and crossover arms below use it. Its geomean of
0.994 is a selected figure: the family was the best of four on those 13 designs and is reported on
them (and on the 2 added later), so it is biased low, and it lies inside the band of the other arms. Its hard arm (one region per cluster, scale~1) is 1.141 in wirelength over
12 designs, better than the baseline on 2; round one's hard arm pooled to 1.088 (all families, all designs). That figure is over the 12 survivors only, and the 3 designs that failed are among the largest, so
survivors flatter it. Round one's Urd hard arm over the same 12 designs is 1.042 (better on 3):
on equal designs the bitwise family's hard arm is worse than Urd's. The hard arm failed in VPR's initial placement on 3 designs
(stereovision0, stereovision1, stereovision2: no free sites for the constrained blocks, the same failure round one
had on blob\_merge) and those are left out. The builder's region check counts a box as large enough when it holds as many logic sites
as the cluster's own CLBs; it does not count the unseeded CLBs that this family leaves at their analytic positions,
which can sit inside a box, so these failures may be builder-side rather than a property of hard regions. Its crossover sweep, started from its own scale-1 placement, has median crossover scale
16 against 16 for round one's best-of-seven $P^*$ over the 13 of 15 designs where
it won at some scale (at least round one's on 11 of 13); at twice the crossover the retention median is
0.20 and the seed beats an unseeded run at the same temperature on 5 of 13 designs. stereovision3 and mkPktMerge had no winning scale at all and are left out of
those medians and counts (round one won up to 256$\times$ on stereovision3 and 16$\times$ on mkPktMerge). Counting them as failures, the family is at least round one's on
11 of 15 designs, and the median over all 15 (a failure taken as below the lowest scale) is
16 against 16.

\textbf{RQ-N4, clustering versus coarse layout.} H4 is not supported. For Urd the wirelength
ratios are random start 0.997, permuted cluster positions 1.020, quotient
layout 1.029; for \code{words\_partial} they are 0.997, 0.980 and 0.994. The analytic baseline is 1 by definition, so a random start below 1 means the anneal from a random start ended with shorter wire than from VPR's analytic start; the gap between the permuted and the quotient arm is what
the coarse layout is worth beyond the clustering itself. Permuting the cluster positions changes the wirelength by
-0.9\% for Urd and -1.4\% for
\code{words\_partial} relative to the quotient layout (negative is shorter).

\textbf{RQ-N1, does VPR rediscover the hierarchy?} H1 is inconclusive. Table~\ref{tab:r2n1}
gives medians over designs: the final placement is above the random null in NMI on 8 of 11
designs for modules and 9 of 15 for words, and in compactness on 10 of 11
and 14 of 15 (more than two null standard deviations). Modules are counted over 11 designs, not 15 or 13:
\code{diffeq1} and \code{diffeq2} have a single module, whose second label is the artificial flat group, and \code{sha} and
\code{stereovision3} have none. Label sets with fewer than 20 labelled CLBs carry little information (module labels on ch\_intrinsics; word labels on ch\_intrinsics, stereovision3, mkDelayWorker32B). Final and analytic placements are close, and the final
NMI is at least the analytic one on 5 of 11 (modules) and 7 of 15
(words) designs. With many small labels the NMI of a random placement is itself high (word null
0.66), so compactness is the cleaner signal.

Graph size is a confounder. The measure $z(Q)$ rises with the number of edges ($\rho=0.85$ ($p<0.001$)), and the gains fall with it: the best
new-family gain against edges is $\rho=-0.60$ ($p=0.017$), the best round-one gain $\rho=-0.29$ ($p=0.289$), Urd's $\rho=-0.37$ ($p=0.177$). Table~\ref{tab:r2n2p}
repeats the correlations of Table~\ref{tab:r2n2} as partial Spearman correlations with the rank of the edge count regressed out of both
variables. The null graphs behind $z(Q)$ use 10 double edge swaps per edge on every design (an earlier run capped the swaps at 200000, which left
3.8 to 9.8 per edge on the five graphs above 20000 edges; the cap was removed and the null recomputed). The best-of-$k$ gains are maxima over $k$
families per design ($k=7$ round one, $k=4$ round two), a positively biased gain, and the noise of a single VPR seed per configuration is unquantified.
