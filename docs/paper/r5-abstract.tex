
A fifth round builds the yardstick the earlier rounds lacked: 72 generated designs whose optimal clustering is known by construction (blocks that each fill one CLB, wired as chains, meshes, trees and random sparse graphs, 4 to 1024 blocks), in the manner of the PEKO/TPEKO placement benchmarks, at two levels: written directly as a netlist, and as Verilog through Odin III. The optimum is exact for the CLB count only; the cut is an upper bound on the minimum cut, and the wirelength and delay ratios are against the placement VPR produces from the forced block clustering, not against a proven placement optimum. Against that clustering the stock VPR packer's gap is CLB count x1.073, wirelength x1.220, critical-path delay x1.093 at the netlist level and CLB count x1.073, wirelength x1.219, critical-path delay x1.082 at the HDL level; the HDL-aware packer's is CLB count x1.031, wirelength x1.099, critical-path delay x1.032. Status under the literal, pre-data rules: the netlist exactness check (H28) is not supported (37 of 72 designs pass the zero-violation rule; the slack rule chosen after the data passes 72 of 72, labelled a variant) and the HDL yardstick check (H18) is not supported (32 of 72 under the original label rule; the amended rule keeps 63, inconclusive). The HDL-aware packer's gain over the stock packer (wirelength x0.902) is a leak, not evidence of HDL knowledge: it sits in \code{dp} (wirelength x0.834), where the word file is the block partition (purity 0.9998 against the stock packer's 0.9501), and is zero in \code{ctl} (x1.000; 27 of 27 \code{.net} files equal the stock files), so this round is no evidence of an HDL-knowledge gain beyond the leak.
